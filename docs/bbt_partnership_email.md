**What I Have Built**

I have developed an application called **Anugamana** (अनुगमन — *to be guided along the path*), an AI-powered spiritual guidance platform built entirely and exclusively on Srila Prabhupada's *Bhagavad Gita As It Is*.

The core experience: a person describes a life situation, an emotion, or a question in plain language — in English, Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, or any of 10 Indian languages — and the application finds the most relevant verse from the Gita, plays the Sanskrit recitation aloud in correct pronunciation, and offers guidance drawn faithfully and exclusively from Prabhupada's purports.

A concrete example:

> *"I keep failing at work and feel like giving up"*
> → Anugamana surfaces Bg 2.47, plays the Sanskrit verse with correct Devanagari pronunciation, shows Prabhupada's translation and purport, and offers guidance drawn directly from his commentary — nothing added, nothing invented.

How the sacred content is handled:

- **The AI does not invent or add to Prabhupada's teachings.** Every guidance note is explicitly constrained to use only the retrieved verse and purport. If the verse does not address the question, the application says so honestly.
- **Full attribution on every result** — verse reference, Srila Prabhupada's name, and *Bhagavad Gita As It Is* are displayed prominently.
- **Sanskrit recitation is authentic** — I use Sarvam AI, a specialised Indian AI system trained on Indic scripts, which handles Sanskrit pronunciation correctly. Western TTS systems mispronounce Sanskrit systematically.
- **Multilingual support is intentional and central** — the Gita's primary audience is Indian. A devotee in rural Maharashtra or Tamil Nadu who thinks in Marathi or Tamil deserves access to these teachings in their own language, not only in English.

---

**The Technical Approach (Why This Is Different)**

I want to be direct about the technical depth of this project, because I believe it distinguishes Anugamana from the several shallow "Gita chatbots" that have appeared recently.

The central challenge I solved: a person types *"I'm paralysed by fear of making the wrong decision"* — this needs to match verse 2.47, which says *"You have a right to perform your duties, not to the fruits."* These share no vocabulary. Standard search fails completely.

My solution: before building the search system, I ran a deep systematic analysis of all 18 chapters using AI — studying the thematic structure, emotional landscape, philosophical concepts, and what makes each chapter unique. I then used that analysis to generate, for every one of the 700 verses, a set of modern-language semantic fields describing: real situations where a person would need this verse, the core teaching in plain English, the emotions it addresses, and the philosophical concepts it introduces. These fields are what the system searches — not the raw Sanskrit text.

This is not a ChatGPT wrapper around the Gita. It is a carefully engineered retrieval system that was designed to serve Prabhupada's purports faithfully and accessibly.

**The Research Contribution**

This project has attracted serious academic attention. I am currently preparing research papers for submission to peer-reviewed venues including SIGIR (the top information retrieval conference) and ACL (the top natural language processing conference).

The primary research contribution — using AI-generated semantic enrichment to bridge the vocabulary gap between modern everyday language and ancient Sanskrit commentary — is a novel methodology that does not yet exist in the academic literature. When published, this research will:

- Establish academic recognition of Prabhupada's *Bhagavad Gita As It Is* as a serious object of computational study
- Demonstrate that Vedic scripture is amenable to the most rigorous modern retrieval techniques
- Be cited by other researchers working on sacred text AI — bringing ongoing academic attention to Srila Prabhupada's books
- Potentially open a path for expanding the same methodology to the Srimad Bhagavatam and Sri Caitanya Caritamrta

A research paper that cites *Bhagavad Gita As It Is* as its primary corpus and demonstrates the depth of its wisdom through rigorous quantitative evaluation is, I believe, a form of distribution that Prabhupada would have appreciated.
