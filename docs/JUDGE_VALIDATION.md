# Judge validation protocol

**Status: not yet performed. No faithfulness number from this system may be
published, quoted in a paper, or shown as a quality claim until it is.**

`app/services/judge.py` scores generated guidance for groundedness, relevance and
restraint. `scripts/pool_and_judge.py` grades verse relevance for the benchmark.
Both are language models. Neither has been checked against a human.

---

## What was wrong before 2026-08-31

The original judge could not do the job it was asked to do.

| Defect | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Asked whether guidance was faithful **to the commentary**, but was only ever passed the **translation** | "Faithful" was unanswerable as posed. The purport never reached the judge. |
| Claude Haiku graded Claude Haiku's own output | Self-preference bias, a well-documented effect. The generator was grading itself. |
| `json.loads` on raw model output | Any preamble or code fence produced a parse failure that was swallowed, silently yielding a null score. |
| Single sample, temperature unset | No measure of the judge's own variance. |
| No human comparison at any point | The central problem. Everything above is fixable; this one is the reason no number can be cited. |

The first three are fixed. The fourth and fifth are what this document is for.

---

## Why model agreement is not enough

`data/benchmark/agreement.json` reports Krippendorff's alpha across three
annotators. That number measures **consistency**, not **correctness**.

All three annotators are Claude models. They grade independently in the sense
that none sees another's output, but they share training data with each other —
and with the system being evaluated. Their errors are therefore correlated, and
alpha overstates true reliability. Three models confidently agreeing on a wrong
grade produces a high alpha and a wrong benchmark.

This is not a hypothetical concern for this corpus specifically: the Bhagavad-gita
and centuries of commentary on it are in every frontier model's training data, so
a model's sense of "the verse that answers this" is itself shaped by the same
tradition the system is retrieving from.

## The protocol

### 1. Relevance judgments (benchmark quality)

```bash
python scripts/annotate.py --annotator <name> --sample 80
```

- **At least two human annotators**, on an overlapping subset, so
  human–human agreement can be reported alongside human–model agreement.
- The sample is stratified across the model's own grades. Judging only its 3s
  would measure precision and reveal nothing about what it wrongly rejected.
- Annotators never see the model's grade.
- Minimum for publication: **150 items**, at least **50 judged by both** humans.

```bash
python scripts/annotate.py --report
```

Reports human–human Krippendorff alpha and human-vs-model quadratic-weighted
kappa.

**Acceptance criterion.** Weighted kappa against humans of **0.6 or above** is the
floor for using the silver qrels to support a claim, and the figure must be
reported in the paper whatever it turns out to be. Below 0.6, the model
judgments describe the models, not the task, and the evaluation must move to
fully human qrels on a smaller query set.

### 2. Faithfulness judgments (generation quality)

Not yet built as a CLI. The protocol:

- Sample **150 generated responses** across query types, including the
  low-confidence and degraded-stage cases, which is where unsupported claims are
  most likely.
- For each, a human sees the query, the verse, **the same commentary window the
  generator received**, and the guidance — then scores groundedness, relevance
  and restraint on the same 1–5 scale the model uses, and marks any claim they
  cannot trace to the provided text.
- Report Spearman correlation and mean absolute error per dimension.
- Report the model judge's own test–retest variance by scoring a 30-item subset
  three times.

**Acceptance criterion.** Spearman **0.5 or above** per dimension, and the judge
must not systematically score its own family's output higher — check by having
the judge grade guidance generated by a non-Claude model on the same inputs.

### 3. What to report in the paper regardless of outcome

- The number of human-judged items and who judged them.
- Human–human agreement.
- Human–model agreement.
- The disagreement pattern: where the model is systematically generous or harsh.
- An explicit statement that the full benchmark is silver, with the human subset
  quantifying how far that can be trusted.

## Known bias to check for specifically

**Famous-verse bias.** The parametric baseline P0 returns 2.47 first for
essentially every anxiety-about-outcomes query. If the model annotators share
that prior, they will grade famous verses generously regardless of fit, which
would systematically favour whichever condition also surfaces famous verses.

The check: compare model and human grades split by verse citation frequency
(2.47, 18.66, 4.7, 9.22 and similar against the long tail). A gap between model
and human grades that widens with fame is the signature of this bias, and it
would need correcting before any ranking claim stands.
